7. Key Insights
Create a section titled Key Insights using cards.
Use these 5 insight cards:
1. Mixed-use spaces create safety anxiety
Pedestrians, cyclists, and skateboarders often share unclear spaces, forcing users to constantly watch for one another and negotiate movement informally.
2. Bike lane continuity is inconsistent
Some bike lanes abruptly stop, merge into sidewalks, or fail to clearly reconnect with roads or protected bike corridors.
3. Right-of-way is unclear
Many users do not know who should yield at intersections or shared crossing points because signage and markings are inconsistent.
4. Weather and maintenance amplify problems
Snow, ice, poor lighting, and uncleared bike lanes make existing infrastructure less safe and less reliable.
5. Users compensate for system gaps
Students adapt by slowing down, dismounting, avoiding certain routes, yielding defensively, or planning extra travel time. This shows that the system depends too much on individual caution instead of clear design.

9. Design Opportunity
Add a section called Design Opportunity.
Use this text:
Instead of treating safety problems as isolated behavior issues, this project reframes them as a system design problem. The opportunity is to make campus movement more predictable by clarifying space ownership, improving visual communication, and reducing points where bikes, skateboards, pedestrians, and vehicles unexpectedly overlap.

10. Final Design Proposal
Create a solution section titled Campus Mobility Improvement Strategy.
Include these solution cards:
1. Reroute high-conflict bike lanes
Focus on areas near WALC, Engineering Fountain, Lawson, and other high-traffic academic zones where bike lanes intersect awkwardly with pedestrian movement.
2. Separate pedestrian and rider flows
Use clearer zoning, color-coded lanes, and physical or visual separation where possible.
3. Improve signage and right-of-way clarity
Add visible stop, yield, and slow-down signs instead of relying only on ground markings.
4. Redesign high-risk crossings
Highlight intersections and transition points where bikes, skateboards, and pedestrians overlap.
5. Improve weather resilience and maintenance
Keep bike lanes clear of snow, improve lighting, and maintain road surfaces to reduce seasonal safety risks.
